How to Prepare and Hoist a Gudi at Home

An easy-to-follow guide for families on how to put together, decorate, and showcase a traditional Gudi at home for Gudi Padwa.

Gudi Padwa Hub
Why this guide matters

The festival is made unforgettable by performing various actions such as raising the Gudi, listening to the panchang, sharing a ritual meal, creating rangoli, and including children, with these instructions emphasizing both significance and practicality.

What you need

Gather a fresh bamboo or strong pole, colorful cloth, neem or mango leaves, flower garland, sugar garland (if preferred), and a light metal vessel for the top. Don't forget sturdy cotton string or a secure tie.

Select items that align with your family traditions. Purchasing extravagant materials is unnecessary when more practical, reusable options already hold sentimental value in your household.

Step 1: prepare the space

Prepare the entrance, balcony, terrace, or window for displaying the Gudi. Often families create a rangoli and set up a small puja thali with kumkum, turmeric, flowers, rice, and a lamp nearby.

Before assembling the Gudi on the balcony, make sure to choose a secure location for tying it. Plan for safety beforehand, rather than improvising after the structure is already built.

Step 2: dress the pole

Hang the bright cloth near the top of the bamboo like a banner, securing it tightly and neatly. Decorate with neem or mango leaves and a flower garland around the same upper area.

When using a sugar garland, position it to be visible without adding too much weight to the top.

Step 3: crown the Gudi

Position the metal container upside down on top of the pole to ensure it is secure and won't slip off. Opt for a lighter vessel over a heavier one, especially in higher areas or when it's windy.

The finished Gudi should appear tall and festive while still being sturdy enough to be raised without any wobbling.

Step 4: raise, secure and worship

Position the Gudi upright by the entrance or in a noticeable spot, securing it to a railing or sturdy support. Typically, the Gudi is placed at a height where it can be easily seen.

Families can present flowers, kumkum, akshata, incense, or a lamp before conducting a brief prayer or aarti in accordance with their household customs. Please ensure that open flames are kept away from fabric, foliage, and breezy areas such as balconies.

A simple child-friendly version

Kids can create a mini Gudi for tabletop display with a small wooden stick, reusable cloth, paper or fabric flowers, and a lightweight cup. This is perfect for smaller spaces like apartments, classrooms, and diaspora events where a full-size bamboo structure isn't feasible.

The significance of the teaching opportunity outweighs its size: clarify the importance of each aspect and involve the child in organizing it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Where should a Gudi be placed?

Typically, it is placed prominently by the entrance, window, balcony, or terrace, in accordance with the customs of the home and family.

Does the vessel have to be copper?

Several families opt for a copper or metal vessel, however, traditions differ. Select a secure, lightweight alternative that aligns with your customs.

Can apartment residents make a small Gudi?

Certainly. A compact or tabletop Gudi can maintain the symbolism in situations where space or building regulations restrict a full-height display.

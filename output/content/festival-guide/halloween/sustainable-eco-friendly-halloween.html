Sustainable Spookiness: An Eco-Friendly Halloween Guide

Halloween is a great time to get creative, making it simple to minimize waste. The most eco-friendly decorations and costumes are typically made from items you already have, borrowed, fixed, or can reuse in the future.

Start with what already exists

Consider shopping your closet, swapping with friends, visiting a thrift store, or reusing pieces from past costumes before purchasing a new one. By using a basic black outfit, old sheet, cardboard, scarves, and accessories, you can easily create multiple recognizable costumes with some creative styling.

Decorate with reusable and compostable materials

Opt for fabric bunting, paper silhouettes, natural branches, pumpkins, gourds, and reusable lights instead of disposable plastic. Keep durable items in labeled bins for a yearly decoration tradition, rather than a one-time purchase.

Make pumpkins part of the food cycle

If a pumpkin is clean and safe to eat, use the flesh or seeds as needed; otherwise, compost it following local regulations. Remove any candles, paint, glitter, or artificial decorations before composting.

Rethink treats and energy use

Purchase candy in sizes suitable for anticipated foot traffic to minimize excess, incorporate a variety of non-edible treats, and utilize timed LED lighting. The most effective sustainability practices are those that can be easily maintained and repeated annually.

Frequently asked questions

Are artificial decorations always worse than natural ones?

It is not always the case. Opting for a lasting decoration that can be used for years is often better than constantly purchasing disposable options. The durability of an item is important.

Can painted pumpkins be composted?

It varies depending on the type of paint and the composting regulations in your area. The best option for composting is plain, untreated pumpkin material.

Pairing candy and wine is most successful when approached as a fun tasting activity, rather than following strict guidelines. Key factors to consider are sweetness, acidity, chocolate level, saltiness, and texture. Opt for small portions and focus on combinations that bring pleasure rather than perfection.

Match sweetness before complexity

Dry wines can come off as harsh when paired with sugary candy, while dessert wines, off-dry styles, and fruit-forward wines are better suited for sweet pairings compared to lean, tannic reds.

Chocolate likes different partners

Soft, fruity wines usually pair well with milk chocolate, while dark chocolate complements deeper flavors but can clash with high tannins. White chocolate, on the other hand, is rich and sweet, making it an interesting contrast with aromatic or lightly sparkling styles.

Caramel, nuts and salt change the equation

Adding salt can help reduce the bitter taste, while caramel and peanut flavors can bring out nutty or oxidative undertones. Experiment by tasting a candy with two different wines to see how the flavors evolve.

Keep the tasting clearly adult

This activity is exclusively for adults who are of legal drinking age. Offer water, food, and nonalcoholic options, and avoid turning a fun pairing into a drinking obligation.

Frequently asked questions

Is there one best wine for Halloween candy?

Different candy recipes and individual preferences can differ greatly. Let sweetness, acidity, and intensity be your reference points rather than strict guidelines.

Can I run the same tasting without alcohol?

Sure. Experiment with sparkling grape juice, tea, cold brew, spiced cider, or botanical zero-proof drinks to see how sweetness and acidity complement different types of candy.

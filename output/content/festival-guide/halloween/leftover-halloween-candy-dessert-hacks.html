Upcycled Leftover Halloween Candy: 10 Dessert and Baking Ideas

The day after Halloween can result in an odd pantry dilemma: an abundance of small candies with different flavors. Treat them as mix-ins for baking. Start by sorting them by texture and flavor, then mix together candies that are alike instead of using them all in one recipe.

Chop chocolate into baking mix-ins

Use mini chocolate bars instead of chocolate chips in cookies, blondies, or brownies. For easier chopping and less smearing, briefly freeze soft or caramel-filled pieces beforehand.

Build bark and snack mixes

Melt chocolate, spread it in a thin layer and sprinkle with chopped candy, pretzels or nuts before it hardens. Popcorn blends nicely with small candies to maintain a harmonious mix of sweet and salty flavors.

Use candies as toppings, not just ingredients

Crushed candies are a versatile topping that can be used to finish off cupcakes, ice cream, milkshakes, or a basic sheet cake. Sprinkling them on top not only adds visual appeal for the eater but also helps to identify and avoid allergens.

Store what you cannot use immediately

Stored properly, wrapped candies can maintain their freshness for a long time as per package instructions, while certain baked goods can be successfully frozen. It is best to discard any unwrapped, damaged, or questionable treats instead of attempting to salvage them in a recipe.

Frequently asked questions

Can leftover candy be frozen?

Certain candies freeze more effectively than others. Always refer to the manufacturer's instructions when possible and be prepared for variations in texture for candies containing caramel, wafers, or fillings.

What is the easiest leftover candy recipe?

Mixing chocolate into bark or cookies is an easy way to combine multiple candies without much planning.

Trick-or-Treating Through the Ages: Souling, Guising and Candy Runs

Trick-or-treating today is as easy as putting on a costume, visiting neighbors' homes, and gathering candy. But its origins are far more fascinating, blending traditional door-to-door practices with community initiatives to transform Halloween pranks into a meaningful family tradition.

Souling and seasonal visiting

During medieval and early modern Europe, less fortunate individuals and children would occasionally go from house to house during All Souls' observances, requesting food in exchange for prayers or songs. These traditions differed depending on the region and should not be viewed as a singular direct predecessor, but they do indicate a long-standing tradition of ritual visitation during this time of year.

Guising brings performance to the doorstep

In Scotland and Ireland, individuals known as "guisers" would don disguises and entertain with songs, jokes, recitations, or tricks in exchange for food or coins. The focus on performance is why costumes and doorstep exchanges became common in Halloween traditions.

The North American neighborhood ritual

As Halloween gained popularity in the US and Canada, communities began to host parties, parades, and activities for children. In the 20th century, candy became more affordable and widely available, suburban areas grew, and trick-or-treating became the familiar tradition we know today.

Making the tradition more inclusive

Numerous neighborhoods have adopted allergy-conscious treats, non-food alternatives, sensory-friendly hours, and "trunk-or-treat" events in order to maintain the traditional social aspect of Halloween while ensuring a safer and more inclusive experience for all families.

Frequently asked questions

When did “trick or treat” become a common phrase?

The expression gained prominence in newspapers and popular culture in North America in the early 1900s, and its popularity grew significantly in the years following World War II.

Is trick-or-treating the same everywhere?

The timing, age expectations, candy customs, and community rules differ greatly depending on the country, city, and neighborhood.

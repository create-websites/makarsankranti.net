The association between Halloween and the jack-o’-lantern is undeniable, but the origins of this tradition can be traced back to Irish folklore, where tales of Stingy Jack and mysterious lights inspired the carved lantern traditions that eventually made their way to America from across the Atlantic.

Who was Stingy Jack?

Different versions of folklore depict Jack as a cunning and self-serving trickster who outsmarts the Devil through bargaining. In death, he is cursed to wander with only a glowing coal to light his way, as he is denied a peaceful resting place. Due to its oral tradition, there is no definitive version of the story.

From wandering lights to carved faces

Throughout Britain and Ireland, eerie lights spotted above marshes were occasionally attributed to tales of restless souls. Locals crafted lanterns out of turnips, mangel-wurzels, or other root vegetables, often carved with spooky faces that added a playful, protective, or theatrical touch to autumn festivities.

Why pumpkins took over

In North America, immigrants discovered big, plentiful pumpkins that were easier to carve. The pumpkin lantern soon became both useful and striking. By the late 1800s and early 1900s, illustrated magazines, postcards, and local events popularized the orange jack-o'-lantern as a symbol of Halloween.

A tradition that keeps reinventing itself

Contemporary carving can vary from basic geometric shapes to detailed portraits and intricate sculptures. Despite the differences in style, the tradition continues to include essential elements such as fresh produce, a flickering light, a face, darkness, and a narrative intended for sharing.

Frequently asked questions

Was Stingy Jack a historical person?

He should be viewed as a figure of folklore, with various versions of the tale being passed down through word of mouth and evolving over the years.

Why are jack-o’-lanterns carved with scary faces?

Faces can draw inspiration from traditional masking and lantern customs, but contemporary styles can vary from spooky to whimsical and ornate.

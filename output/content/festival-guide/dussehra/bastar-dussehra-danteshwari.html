Bastar Dussehra: The 75-Day Festival of Goddess Danteshwari

Discover the 75-day tribal festival of Bastar Dussehra, focused on Goddess Danteshwari, community involvement, and regional ritual customs as outlined in the editorial.

Dussehra Hub
Why this guide matters

Bastar Dussehra breaks the traditional mold of Dussehra celebrations, focusing instead on Goddess Danteshwari and the unique rituals of the Bastar region.

India’s unusually long Dussehra cycle

The Bastar Dussehra celebration spans 75 days, distinguishing it as one of the lengthiest festival cycles linked to Dussehra. Instead of condensing all activities into one evening, the rituals progress gradually over time.

The extended timeline mirrors a sacred calendar unique to the region, encompassing its own reasoning and communal duties.

Goddess Danteshwari at the center

In contrast to the Rama-Ravana narrative in North India, Bastar Dussehra honors Goddess Danteshwari, reflecting the region's unique blend of local goddess traditions, tribal involvement, and historical institutions.

It is advisable for visitors to refrain from regarding it as a version of Ravana Dahan.

Community labor and ritual participation

The success of large public festivals relies on various contributions such as processional work, ritual roles, preparation, music, craft, and collective coordination. Bastar’s celebration serves as a valuable example of how community involvement transforms into sacred tradition.

The event demonstrates how regional identity is maintained through regular public rituals.

How to approach as a visitor

Visitors should prioritize cultural sensitivity, follow local guidance, and obtain consent before taking photographs. Ritual spaces should be respected as sacred places, not as tourist attractions.

A meaningful visit prioritizes listening and understanding over collecting experiences.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Is Bastar Dussehra about Ravana Dahan?

The festival revolves around Goddess Danteshwari and the unique traditions of Bastar.

Why is it described as 75 days?

The editorial brief describes it as a lengthy ritual cycle that spans about 75 days.

Where is it celebrated?

It is associated with the Bastar region of Chhattisgarh.

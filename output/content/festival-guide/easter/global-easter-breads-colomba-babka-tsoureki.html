Global Easter Breads: Colomba, Babka and Tsoureki

Explore the traditions surrounding three festive breads: Italian colomba, Polish babka, and Greek tsoureki.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCulinary & Baking Trends

Colomba di Pasqua: the dove-shaped Italian bread

Colomba is an Italian Easter bread that is rich and shaped like a dove, often adorned with almonds and a crispy sugar topping. While it shares similarities with panettone in terms of its enriched dough, Colomba has a distinctive seasonal character all its own.

Polish babka: tall, rich and celebratory

Babka varies in appearance throughout Eastern Europe, with Polish Easter versions typically being tall, buttery cakes or yeast breads infused with citrus, vanilla, or raisins and topped with glaze or sugar.

Greek tsoureki: aromatic braided bread

Tsoureki, a tender braided bread traditionally enjoyed during Greek Easter, is infused with aromatic ingredients like mahlepi and mastic, with red-dyed eggs often nestled within the braid as a festive Easter decoration.

Bake the tradition, not just the shape

Every type of bread is rooted in a rich cultural and religious background. When trying a new bread, look for recipes from chefs who share the regional ingredients and family traditions, rather than solely relying on its appearance.

Frequently asked questions

Are these breads interchangeable?

They each have unique doughs, aromatics, shapes, and cultural backgrounds, despite being commonly seen during Easter celebrations.

Why are eggs sometimes baked into tsoureki?

Red eggs are a popular symbol of Greek Easter and can be used as decoration on the braided loaf.

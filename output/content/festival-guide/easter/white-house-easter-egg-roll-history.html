White House Easter Egg Roll: History and Traditions

Follow the evolution of the South Lawn tradition from 1878 with a blend of music, egg races, souvenir eggs, and contemporary family events.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterFamily, Community & Modern Traditions

Why the White House lawn became the setting

The annual White House Easter Egg Roll was established in 1878 by President Rutherford B. Hayes, after Congress restricted public use of the U.S. Capitol grounds where egg rolling was already a popular Easter Monday activity in Washington.

A tradition that kept evolving

Over time, the U.S. Marine Band joined the festivities in the nineteenth century, and egg-roll races became a popular activity at the event.

Souvenir eggs and family programming

The Reagan White House launched a search for wooden eggs autographed by famous figures in 1981. These painted eggs have since become cherished mementos, with current initiatives now featuring storytelling, performances, and interactive stations.

Why the event matters beyond politics

The Egg Roll, a beloved civic family tradition held at a historic political site, remains popular due to the timeless concept of children coming together on the South Lawn for spring festivities. Despite challenges such as war, renovations, inclement weather, and public-health crises, the event has persevered.

Frequently asked questions

When was the first annual White House Easter Egg Roll?

The White House Historical Association established the tradition of the first annual White House Easter Egg Roll on April 22, 1878.

Who opened the White House grounds to egg rollers?

In 1878, President Rutherford B. Hayes allowed children to access the grounds.

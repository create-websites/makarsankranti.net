Paschal Lamb Symbolism in Easter Tradition

Discover the reasons behind the presence of lamb in scripture, worship, art, and Easter meals, and explore the nuances of its symbolism compared to its role in cuisine

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterHistory, Folklore & Spiritual Origins

The biblical layers behind the symbol

The lamb holds multiple biblical significances, with Passover evoking memories of the Exodus lamb and Christian scriptures associating lamb imagery with Jesus, notably as the Lamb of God. As a result, Easter has adopted a symbol steeped in sacrifice, redemption, and new covenant theology.

From altar and artwork to the dinner table

Lamb is a popular Easter centerpiece in certain regions, while in others it is seen as a shaped cake, butter sculpture, or decorative motif. Although food traditions and theology are intertwined, they are not interchangeable.

Regional differences matter

Lamb is a staple in Mediterranean, Central European, and Middle Eastern Christian food traditions, while many North American families opt for ham or another main dish. The religious significance of an Easter meal is not determined by the protein choice.

Using the symbol thoughtfully

Before using the Paschal Lamb as a decoration at a church meal, classroom, or family gathering, take a moment to share its significance in the wider Holy Week story, connecting food and art for all to appreciate.

Frequently asked questions

Is lamb required for Easter dinner?

Lamb is a common tradition in numerous cultures, however Easter feasts can differ greatly.

What does “Paschal” mean?

Paschal is linked to both Passover and Easter, stemming from terminology associated with the Christian and Jewish Passover customs.

Holy Week Explained: From Palm Sunday to Easter

An easy-to-follow outline of Palm Sunday, Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, Holy Saturday, and Easter Sunday.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterHistory, Folklore & Spiritual Origins

Palm Sunday: entry into Jerusalem

The beginning of Holy Week is marked by Palm Sunday, commemorating Jesus' arrival in Jerusalem and the enthusiastic reception from the crowds. Some churches incorporate palm branches into their worship services, while others may use local greenery as a substitute in areas where palms are not readily available.

Maundy Thursday: meal, service and humility

Maundy Thursday is a day to commemorate the Last Supper and, in various customs, the act of Jesus washing his disciples' feet. Observances often feature elements like communion, foot washing, the symbolic stripping of the altar, or a meditative vigil lasting into the night.

Good Friday and Holy Saturday

Good Friday emphasizes the crucifixion with a solemn tone, incorporating traditions such as readings of the Passion, veneration of the cross, and extended prayer. Holy Saturday is a day of anticipation, often culminating in the Easter Vigil after nightfall in many churches.

Easter Sunday: resurrection and joy

Easter Sunday is marked by celebrations of resurrection through music, flowers, light, and communal worship. From sunrise services to cathedral liturgies to small local gatherings, all signify the transition from mourning to hope.

When attending a new church, be sure to review local service times and dress codes; Holy Week traditions can differ greatly among different denominations and cultures.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Good Friday and Easter Sunday?

Good Friday commemorates the crucifixion; Easter Sunday celebrates the resurrection.

Why is Holy Week called holy?

It condenses the key events of the Christian Passion story into a series of days centered around prayer and worship.

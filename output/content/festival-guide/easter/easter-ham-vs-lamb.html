Easter Ham vs. Lamb: Choosing the Holiday Centerpiece

Contrast two traditional Easter centerpieces based on taste, meaning, preparation method, diner preferences, and remaining food.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🌼 EasterCulinary & Baking Trends

Why families choose ham

Ham is a favorite choice for Easter dinners in North America due to its ability to serve a large group, be made in advance, and complement various sweet or tangy flavors. Leftover bone-in ham can also be repurposed for sandwiches, soups, and breakfast options.

Why families choose lamb

Lamb holds significant symbolic and culinary importance during Passover and Easter in various cultures. A roasted leg or shoulder of lamb pairs perfectly with garlic, rosemary, lemon, and herbs, while those in smaller households may opt for chops instead.

Choose based on your table, not a rule

Consider the preferences of your guests, the available oven space, your budget, and the variety of side dishes you plan to serve. Opting for a mixed table setup may allow for a smaller roast to accompany a vegetarian centerpiece, instead of selecting a single large meat option.

Plan leftovers before you cook

Ham can be transformed into biscuits, strata, or bean soup, while lamb can be made into sandwiches, grain bowls, or shepherd’s pie bakes. It is crucial to cool and refrigerate leftovers promptly, prioritizing food safety over trying to stretch out one more meal.

Frequently asked questions

Is lamb more traditional than ham for Easter?

Lamb has long been connected to religious symbolism, whereas ham is now closely linked with Easter in numerous North American homes. Both represent well-established traditions.

Which is easier for a large group?

Ham is frequently easier because it can be heated and glazed with minimal last-minute preparation.

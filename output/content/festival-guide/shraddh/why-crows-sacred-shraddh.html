Why Crows Are Sacred During Shraddh: Symbolism, Folklore and Ecology

Discover the significance of feeding crows during Shraddh, their role as ancestral messengers in folklore, and how to respectfully carry out this custom in urban areas.

Shraddh Hub
Why this guide matters

In many regions of India, crows play a significant role in the observance of Shraddh. Families often offer food to them and view their presence as a positive omen. This tradition blends ancestral beliefs, cultural stories, and the simple fact that crows have historically coexisted with humans in close proximity.

The messenger symbolism

According to common belief, crows are associated with the Pitrs and can be seen as messengers or receivers of messages from ancestors. Feeding these birds is a way to honor and connect with our family beyond the household.

The symbolism holds significance without necessitating each family to view a crow's actions as a direct supernatural communication.

Why crows fit the ritual landscape

Crows are known for their intelligence, keen observation skills, and remarkable ability to thrive in human habitats. They are quick to spot food and have coexisted with humans in urban and rural settings for centuries.

Their ecological proximity probably enhances their ritual presence: they are nearby, vigilant, and well-known.

Do not turn arrival into anxiety

The absence of a crow may be due to weather, habitat, noise, or local population patterns, and should not be taken as evidence that ancestors refused an offering.

The significance of rituals should not rely on coercing wildlife to act as desired.

Feed wildlife responsibly

Provide limited quantities of suitable food, steer clear of plastic dishes, prevent potential traffic risks, and adhere to local regulations or guidelines. In areas where feeding crows is not feasible, consider contributing to bird rescue or conservation efforts as a way to demonstrate the same level of concern.

The goal is remembrance without ecological harm.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Are crows literally ancestors?

This is a question of faith and tradition. The symbolic rituals are common, but they should not be construed as proven scientific truths.

What if no crow comes to eat?

Do not view it as a form of rejection or bad luck; the behavior of wildlife is influenced by numerous common factors.

Can I substitute another act of care?

Indeed, the practice of responsible bird feeding, providing water stations, or supporting animal welfare can help uphold the ethical values behind it.

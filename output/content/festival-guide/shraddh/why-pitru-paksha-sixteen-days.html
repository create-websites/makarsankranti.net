What is the significance of 16 days? Exploring the lunar and spiritual reasoning behind Pitru Paksha.

Discover the reasons behind Pitru Paksha's duration of the waning moon phase, the significance of tithis in ancestral rituals, and the traditional explanation for its sixteen-day duration.

Shraddh Hub
Why this guide matters

Pitru Paksha occurs during the dark or waning phase of the lunar month, with the exact dates differing yearly due to Hindu traditions following lunar tithis rather than a set Gregorian calendar. This period is commonly known as sixteen days, ending with Sarvapitri Amavasya.

The rhythm of the waning moon

As the fortnight diminishes towards the new moon, Hindu rituals associate this quieter phase with introspection, recollection, and letting go. Pitru Paksha utilizes this lunar cycle to establish a special time dedicated to honoring ancestors.

Since tithis do not align precisely with 24-hour civil days, calendar calculations may vary based on location and year.

Why death tithi matters

Shraddh ceremonies are often observed by families on the tithi of an ancestor's passing, rather than on a set Gregorian date, creating a relational ritual calendar where various days in the fortnight hold special meaning for different family members.

When the specific date is not known, Sarvapitri Amavasya is typically observed as a day to honor all ancestors.

Why the period is called sixteen days

In popular belief, a full ancestral fortnight is typically combined with the concluding new-moon observance within the lunar calendar system, though variations may occur in local calendars.

A local panchang is the best guide for precise observance.

The spiritual purpose of a defined season

Allocating a specific timeframe provides a sense of organization to remembrance. Rather than grief and ancestry remaining ethereal concepts, families are given a designated time frame on the calendar for traditions, tales, meals, giving, and contemplation.

The rhythm of Pitru Paksha can help break the cycle of constant fear that ancestral practices may bring, allowing for a smooth transition into the festive season.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Is Pitru Paksha always exactly 16 calendar days?

Traditionally, it is said to span sixteen days, but local tithi calculations may cause civil dates to vary.

Why is the waning moon important?

The fading two weeks offer a time-honored backdrop for self-reflection, recollection, and honoring of ancestors.

What if I do not know an ancestor’s death tithi?

Sarvapitri Amavasya is typically observed for ancestors whose tithi is unknown or whose rituals were not performed.

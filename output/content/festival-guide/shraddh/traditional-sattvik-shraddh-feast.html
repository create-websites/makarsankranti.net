Traditional Sattvik Shraddh Feast: Kheer, Poori, Arbi, Pumpkin and More

Discover traditional sattvik dishes typically made during Shraddh, such as kheer, poori, arbi, and pumpkin, along with insights on regional differences and easy meal preparation tips.

Shraddh Hub
Why this guide matters

Shraddh meals are typically uncomplicated, sattvik, and influenced by family customs. Common dishes like kheer, poori, arbi, and pumpkin are enjoyed in North Indian homes, with other regions incorporating their unique grains, vegetables, sweets, and dietary guidelines.

Why sattvik food is emphasized

A sattvik meal, which is often free of alcohol and meat and may also exclude onion and garlic, helps maintain a calm and respectful atmosphere for ancestral remembrance in certain households.

The goal is not to restrict culinary options, but rather to maintain clarity, simplicity, and consistency within the ritual environment.

A classic North Indian menu

A typical plate could consist of poori, arbi lightly spiced, pumpkin sabzi, seasonal veggies, dal, rice, and kheer. Ancestors' favorite dishes may also be cooked by families, as long as they adhere to household ritual guidelines.

There is no single pan-Indian Shraddh menu.

Cook with ritual and food safety in mind

Ensure that food is prepared in a sanitary kitchen, portion sizes are calculated accurately, and dairy products are kept refrigerated as necessary. It is essential to prioritize modern food-safety practices when sharing meals with guests, priests, or community members.

Respect for ritual never requires ignoring hygiene.

Food as memory

Family recipes often hold more personal history than official records. A grandmother's special kheer recipe or a grandfather's favorite vegetable dish can bring back vivid memories.

During Pitru Paksha, jot down these recipes to turn the meal into a tribute and record.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions

Is there one required Shraddh menu?

No. Menus vary by region, caste, family and local custom.

Are onion and garlic always prohibited?

Numerous families opt out of sattvik meals, as household customs vary.

Can a favorite family recipe be included?

Frequently, the family's meal rules allow for this, as it can enhance the significance of the ritual.

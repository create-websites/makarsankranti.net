Celebrating Thanksgiving Abroad: An Expat Guide

Celebrate Thanksgiving overseas by adapting recipes with local ingredients, making new friends, working in smaller kitchens, and embracing traditions centered around togetherness rather than perfection.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingGratitude, Wellness & Traditions

Define what matters most to you

In times of pricey or scarce familiar ingredients, prioritize two to three key traditions that capture the essence of Thanksgiving. This could include turkey, cranberry dishes, pumpkin treats, watching football, practicing gratitude, or simply coming together at the dinner table.

Substitute by function, not by exact ingredient

If turkey is not an option, consider using a bird or vegetarian dish that is readily available in your area. Opt for local fruits, squash, chutneys, or preserves as alternatives to American-style sweet potatoes, cranberries, or canned pumpkin to achieve similar sweet, tart, or earthy flavors.

Invite local friends into the tradition

Celebrating Thanksgiving in a different country can transform into a cultural sharing experience rather than just a personal replication of home. Embrace the holiday's true essence by discussing its complex past and encourage guests to contribute a dish or gratitude custom from their own cultural background.

Adapt to smaller kitchens and schedules

Urban apartments might not have enough space for big appliances like ovens or fridges. Opt for smaller cuts of meat, simple one-dish sides, prepare-ahead meals, and potluck items. If you work on Thursdays, consider celebrating on the closest weekend without feeling tied to the calendar.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need turkey for Thanksgiving abroad?

No need to change everything, just stick to the traditions that are important to you and opt for local ingredients for the rest.

How can I explain Thanksgiving to international guests?

Explore the juxtaposition between the contemporary celebration of family and thankfulness during Thanksgiving and the intricate colonial and Indigenous roots of the holiday in the United States.

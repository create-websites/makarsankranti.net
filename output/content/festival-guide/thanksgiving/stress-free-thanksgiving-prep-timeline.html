A Stress-Free Thanksgiving Prep Timeline

Plan your Thanksgiving menu, shop for ingredients, thaw out items, prepare make-ahead dishes, set up your table, and ensure a smooth final cooking day with a two-week preparation timeline.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Two weeks out: lock the shape of the day

Please verify the number of guests, any dietary restrictions, and the desired serving time. Select a menu that is suitable for your home kitchen appliances and cooking space, rather than a menu intended for a professional kitchen.

Delegate responsibilities now. Determine which items guests should contribute, which should be bought, and which should be handmade.

One week out: shop and prep the environment

Purchase shelf-stable items, drinks, serving items, and decorations. Ensure you have roasting pans, thermometers, carving utensils, and storage bins. Make room in the fridge before your last grocery run.

When using a frozen turkey, plan ahead for thawing time. The USDA suggests allowing approximately 24 hours of refrigerator thawing for every four to five pounds of turkey.

Two to three days out: make what improves with time

Get ready to make cranberry sauce, pie dough, various desserts, salad dressings, and bread cubes for stuffing. If your recipe permits, chop up some sturdy vegetables. Also, consider setting the table if it is not being used for regular meals.

Thanksgiving Day: work backward from serving time

Record the oven schedule for each dish, including cooking times and resting periods. Prioritize cooking dishes that can be held for longer periods before cooking more delicate items. Allow for a buffer of time before serving the meal to ensure that carving and gravy preparation do not coincide with guests already seated.

Frequently asked questions

What should I make first?

Begin with items that have a longer shelf life such as cranberry sauce, desserts, bread for drying, and certain casseroles. Save the preparation of fresh vegetables and the final cooking of the turkey for a later time.

How much buffer time should I allow?

Allowing for a minimum of 30 to 60 minutes of free time before serving enables the absorption of slow cooking, reheating, and carving delays.

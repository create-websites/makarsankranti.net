Mindful Eating at the Thanksgiving Table

Savor your Thanksgiving meal at a leisurely pace, staying mindful of your hunger levels and portion sizes, all without letting the joy of the occasion be overshadowed by strict diet guidelines.

Arrive hungry, not ravenous

Not eating meals to “cut calories” can make it difficult to recognize when you're full later on. Starting the day with a regular breakfast or a light lunch sets you up to fully enjoy dinner, rather than feeling rushed to eat.

Survey before serving

Start by surveying the entire spread. Select the dishes that truly appeal to you, including ones that hold sentimental value, and serve yourself small portions at first. Don't hesitate to go back for seconds if desired.

Slow the first ten minutes

At the start of a holiday meal, it's tempting to rush through. Take breaks to chat, feel the textures, and savor the temperatures. Slowing down can enhance the comfort of the meal.

Avoid moral labels for food

Thanksgiving doesn't require categorizing foods as "good," "bad," "clean," or "cheat." Food can nourish, bring pleasure, reflect culture, and create memories all at once. If you have dietary restrictions for medical reasons, consult your clinician; otherwise, being flexible is often more sustainable than feeling guilty.

Frequently asked questions

Should I skip breakfast before Thanksgiving dinner?

Eating a regular meal earlier in the day can help prevent arriving at dinner ravenously hungry, making it easier to enjoy the meal.

What if I eat more than planned?

Instead of punishing yourself or trying to compensate for one big meal, simply return to your regular routine and prioritize your comfort, hydration, and overall wellbeing.

The Friendsgiving Hosting Playbook

Organize a laid-back Friendsgiving with assigned potluck dishes, dietary considerations, drink and dessert stations, seating arrangements, and easy cleanup plans.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Decide what Friendsgiving means for your group

Friendsgiving can take on various formats such as a formal sit-down dinner, buffet, brunch, or casual potluck. Be sure to specify the format in the invitation so guests know what to bring - whether it be a finished dish, ingredients, drinks, or just themselves.

Use categories instead of micromanaging recipes

Encourage variety by assigning categories like mains, vegetables, starches, salads, desserts, and drinks instead of specifying individual dishes. This allows for creativity while ensuring a balanced potluck spread.

Inquire about any food allergies or dietary preferences beforehand. Clearly mark dishes on the table to avoid guests having to question each cook.

Create stations that reduce kitchen traffic

Place beverages in a separate location from the cooking area. Designate a specific area for dessert or coffee service to be opened post-dinner. Arrange plates and utensils at the start of the buffet line and position napkins at both ends.

Make cleanup part of the design

Have empty containers on hand for leftovers and designate specific areas for trash, recycling, and dirty dishes. If everyone is nearby, designate a small team to help with a quick 15-minute cleanup to avoid leaving all the work to the host.

Frequently asked questions

Should the host cook the turkey?

Friendsgiving is successful when the host offers one main dish and guests contribute to the menu, but only if the host is willing.

How do I handle dietary restrictions?

Get the items early, make sure dishes are labeled clearly, and ensure each guest has multiple substantial options.

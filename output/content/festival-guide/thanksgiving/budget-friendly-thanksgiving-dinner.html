Budget-Friendly Thanksgiving Dinner Planning

Create a budget-friendly Thanksgiving feast with strategic menu planning, seasonal produce, store-brand items, potluck contributions, and minimizing waste.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingHosting, Prep & Decor

Set a per-person target before shopping

When planning your holiday grocery shopping, determine your budget by dividing the total amount by the number of guests you will be serving. Make necessary adjustments to your shopping list before heading to the store to avoid overspending.

Choose one centerpiece, not five

Whether it's a turkey, a plant-based centerpiece, or another main dish, you only need one to be the star of the meal. Save money by filling the table with affordable seasonal sides like potatoes, squash, carrots, rolls, beans, and easy salads.

Buy ingredients that work in several dishes

Onions, celery, herbs, stock, flour, butter, and oil are versatile ingredients that can enhance stuffing, gravy, vegetables, and casseroles, helping to minimize waste from partially used packages and overlooked specialty items.

Use potluck logic strategically

If guests wish to pitch in, assign general categories such as dessert, salad, vegetable side, drinks, or bread instead of specific recipes. This helps evenly distribute costs and work, preventing multiple identical casseroles.

Include leftover meal options in your budget. Utilize containers, sandwiches, soup, and meals for the following day to stretch your holiday grocery bill across several days instead of just one large dinner.

Frequently asked questions

Is a whole turkey always the cheapest main?

It is not always the case. Consider the number of guests and prices in your area. Opting for a smaller turkey, turkey breast, plant-based centerpiece, or a potluck main dish may be more budget-friendly.

How do I avoid duplicate potluck dishes?

Categorize and maintain a collective list for guests to view the topics that have been addressed.

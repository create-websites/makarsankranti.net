Thanksgiving Wine Pairings: A Flexible Guide

Pair Thanksgiving's delicious sweet, savory, and herbal flavors with a variety of red, white, and sparkling wines, as well as non-alcoholic options to accommodate all guests.

Why Thanksgiving is a pairing challenge

Thanksgiving features a medley of roasted poultry, herbs, butter, tangy cranberry, sweet squash, gravy, and dessert all on one plate. When selecting wines, opt for options with good acidity, moderate alcohol content, and enough fruit to complement the array of flavors.

White and sparkling styles

White wines with bright acidity can complement rich dishes. Options like Riesling, Chenin Blanc, Pinot Gris, and lightly oaked Chardonnay are great choices based on the dish's sweetness and seasoning. Sparkling wine is a versatile option as its acidity and bubbles can cleanse the palate.

Red styles that stay nimble

Red wines that are lighter to medium-bodied, such as Pinot Noir, Gamay, and certain Grenache-based wines, are typically a better match for turkey than heavily tannic options. They can enhance the flavors of herbs and roasted dishes without being too overpowering.

Dessert and nonalcoholic options

When it comes to pumpkin or apple desserts, opt for a wine that matches the sweetness of the dessert rather than a dry table wine. Nonalcoholic sparkling cider, tea, grape-based sparkling drinks, and botanical sodas can also be paired using the same principle of balancing sweetness, acidity, and spice.

Note: You may choose to include alcohol, but please do so responsibly and offer equally considerate nonalcoholic alternatives.

Frequently asked questions

What wine style is easiest with Thanksgiving?

Vibrant, versatile wines with bright acidity, such as sparkling wine, Riesling, Pinot Noir, and Gamay, are known for their food-friendly qualities.

Should every guest drink wine?

Offering appealing nonalcoholic choices along with water is always a must. The focus should be on enhancing the enjoyment of food, not on making anyone feel pressured to drink alcohol.

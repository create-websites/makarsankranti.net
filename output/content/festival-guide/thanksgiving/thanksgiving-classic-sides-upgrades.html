Upgrading Classic Thanksgiving Sides Without Losing Tradition

Elevate traditional Thanksgiving dishes like stuffing, mashed potatoes, cranberry sauce, and green bean casserole with clever flavor enhancements while maintaining the holiday spirit.

Read the guideAll 20 articles
🍁 ThanksgivingCulinary Guides & Recipes

Keep the memory, change the technique

Holiday side dishes come with high expectations. To update them successfully, it's best to maintain the dish's traditional essence while incorporating a few intentional enhancements. Making too many changes at once can turn your table into a fancy restaurant menu rather than a cozy Thanksgiving spread.

Stuffing: build layers of savory flavor

Make sure to toast the bread well to maintain its texture. Cook the onion and celery until they are sweet, then mix in herbs like sage, thyme, or parsley. To add variety, think about including roasted mushrooms, apples, chestnuts, or toasted nuts. Remember to keep the additional ingredients in proportion so they enhance rather than overpower the flavor of the bread and herbs.

Mashed potatoes: texture first

Select starchy potatoes for a fluffy mash, pat them dry post-boiling, and heat up butter and milk before combining. Steer clear of excessive mixing to prevent a sticky texture. Enhance the dish with brown butter, roasted garlic, or a subtle hint of horseradish for added depth.

Cranberry sauce and green beans

Cranberry sauce pairs well with acidity and spice, such as orange zest, ginger, or a hint of warm spice to enhance the fruit. To freshen up the traditional green bean casserole, swap out canned beans for blanched beans, sautéed mushrooms, and crispy onions, while maintaining the dish's original flavor profile.

Frequently asked questions

How many side dishes should I make?

When serving a variety of dishes, consider preparing three to five generous sides along with bread and cranberry sauce. Adjust the quantities based on the number of guests and the size of your kitchen.

Can sides be made ahead?

Many dishes can be prepared in advance, such as cranberry sauce, casseroles, and stuffing components, while mashed potatoes should be reheated with extra care.

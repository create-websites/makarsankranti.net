Rockefeller Center Christmas Tree: History and Design

The Rockefeller Center tree is not only a massive engineering feat but also a cherished public tradition, turning a single towering spruce into a renowned symbol of the holiday season.

How the tradition began

The tradition of the Rockefeller Center tree can be traced back to the early 1930s when a Christmas tree was first erected by workers at the construction site. This led to an official annual lighting ceremony that quickly became a significant event in New York City.

As time passed, the tree transformed from a simple local decoration to a national holiday symbol due to increased broadcast coverage, skating-rink imagery, and tourism.

How a tree is selected

The selected tree is usually a massive Norway spruce sourced from the area near New York. Criteria for choosing include its appearance, health, ease of removal, and the challenge of moving such a tall tree along public roads.

The ultimate decision isn't solely based on height; symmetry, branch density, and safe movement are equally important considerations.

Transport, rigging and lights

Transporting and setting up a tree of this size necessitates the use of cranes, protective covering, strategic route mapping, and a well-coordinated team. After securing the tree, workers then add elaborate lighting and the star at the top.

While visitors may not notice the engineering behind it, the display's ability to be safely maintained in a busy urban plaza for weeks is largely due to this hidden aspect.

Visiting without the stress

For a quieter experience, consider visiting earlier in the day or during the off-peak season. Be prepared for crowds during peak evenings and consider taking public transportation to avoid traffic congestion.

Instead of attempting to visit every Midtown attraction in one short trip, consider exploring the tree along with the nearby windows, churches, Bryant Park, or Fifth Avenue.

Context note: Before visiting, make sure to check the official Rockefeller Center information as the lighting dates, viewing rules, and security arrangements are subject to change each season.

Frequently asked questions

When did the Rockefeller Center tree tradition begin?

The origins of the tradition can be traced back to the early 1930s, when the annual lighting tradition quickly followed.

What kind of tree is usually used?

A large Norway spruce is the most common choice.

Saint Lucia Day in Sweden: Light, Song and Saffron

On December 13, Lucia celebrations in Sweden are known for using light, music, and hospitality to embody a beloved winter tradition.

Why Lucia is associated with light

Saint Lucia, a Christian saint, is celebrated in Sweden during the darkest part of winter with a modern observance focused on light. The tradition includes a Lucia figure dressed in white and adorned with a crown of candles or electric lights.

Today's celebration combines Christian symbols, local traditions, educational institutions, businesses, choral groups, and family get-togethers instead of simply mirroring a single unaltered medieval ceremony.

The procession and songs

Lucia processions typically feature participants in white robes, star boys, and choirs performing traditional holiday tunes. Various settings like schools, churches, offices, and public spaces may host their own interpretations of the event.

Although real candles may still be used in carefully supervised environments, electric candles are more common for safety reasons, particularly around children.

Lussebullar and the food tradition

Lussebullar or lussekatter, also known as saffron buns, are a traditional Lucia treat often enjoyed with coffee, tea, or other warm drinks during the festival, thanks to their vibrant color and unique curled shapes.

Ginger biscuits and other holiday treats might be enjoyed alongside the buns, but the saffron bread stands out as the most iconic culinary creation of the season.

How to celebrate respectfully

A simple at-home celebration can include baking saffron buns, playing Swedish Lucia music, dimming the lights, and lighting candles. If children are involved, emphasize service, light, and community over perfect costumes.

When you join a public procession, be sure to adhere to any photography guidelines in place and keep in mind that these events often hold significance as community traditions, not just tourist attractions.

Context note: Lucia celebrations differ throughout Sweden and Swedish-speaking communities, with electric candles being the preferred option for children's processions due to their safety.

Frequently asked questions

When is Saint Lucia Day?

It is observed on December 13.

What are lussebullar?

They are saffron-flavored Swedish buns strongly associated with Lucia season.

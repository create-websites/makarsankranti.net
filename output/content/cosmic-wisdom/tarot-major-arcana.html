What tarot is—and how it is read

Tarot is a 78-card symbolic system used to explore situations, choices and inner states. Cards are arranged in spreads, then interpreted through imagery, position and neighboring cards.

◆

A symbolic language

Images can act as metaphors for a situation, feeling, pattern or turning point.

◉

Position matters

A card’s meaning changes with its place in a spread and its relationship to nearby cards.

↕

Upright or reversed

A reversed card is often read as blocked, delayed, internalized or shadowed energy—not simply “bad.”

Deck structure

Major and Minor Arcana

The deck divides into two complementary parts: archetypal turning points and the practical texture of everyday life.

22

Major Arcana

Numbered 0 through 21, these cards symbolize major themes, lessons, choices and transitions. This visual guide covers every one.

56

Minor Arcana

Four suits of 14 cards describe day-to-day circumstances.

Wands · FireAmbition, creativity and action
Cups · WaterEmotion, relationships and intuition
Swords · AirThought, conflict and truth
Pentacles · EarthMoney, work and the material world

Upright

The card’s central meaning expressed outwardly and in its clearest, most direct form.

Reversed

The same energy may be blocked, delayed, internalized or turned toward its shadow side.

Every Card Is a Mirror

The Major Arcana forms one long symbolic arc: 22 ways to ask where you stand, what is changing and what comes next.

The Fool’s Journey

One arc, three movements

The Major Arcana is often read as a symbolic journey from openness, through testing and transformation, toward integration.

Cards 0–VII

Entering the world

The Fool meets will, intuition, care, authority, tradition, relationship and the drive to move forward.

Cards VIII–XIV

Inner development

Strength, solitude, changing fortune, truth, surrender, transformation and balance reshape the inner life.

Cards XV–XXI

Transformation and return

Shadow, upheaval, hope, uncertainty, illumination, reckoning and completion lead back to a new beginning.

i

Interpretive context

Tarot is a symbolic and cultural practice, not a scientifically validated way to predict events or determine facts. Use readings for reflection or entertainment—not as a substitute for medical, legal, financial, mental-health or safety advice. In tarot, the Death card generally symbolizes endings and transformation, not literal death.

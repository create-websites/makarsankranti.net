Stories behind the Festival of Lights

Diwali Stories

Diwali is celebrated through many regional and faith traditions. These seven stories illuminate different paths to the festival while returning to a shared idea: light, freedom, knowledge and devotion can overcome darkness.

A festival with many stories

Diwali does not have a single story or identical observance everywhere. Hindu communities connect the season with Rama, Krishna, Lakshmi, Vamana or Kali; Jains remember Mahavira’s nirvana; and Sikhs mark Bandi Chhor Divas.

The stories vary by region, community and tradition, but lamps appear again and again. Their light becomes a visible language for welcome, liberation, spiritual knowledge and the victory of good.

Seven traditions

Different paths to the Festival of Lights

Each account explains a different meaning attached to the season. Together they show why Diwali is both deeply diverse and instantly recognizable.

One festival, one thread

What ties the stories together

These traditions do not erase one another. They show how communities have found their own language for Diwali’s light: Rama’s homecoming, Krishna’s victory, Lakshmi’s welcome, Bali’s humility, Mahavira’s knowledge, Guru Hargobind Sahib’s commitment to shared freedom and Kali’s protective power.

The common thread is movement from darkness toward light—not only as decoration, but as an ethical and spiritual direction: good over evil, freedom over oppression, knowledge over ignorance and generosity over self-interest.

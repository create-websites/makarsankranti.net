Stories behind the harvest of light

Makar Sankranti Stories

As the sun begins its northward journey, communities across India mark the turning point in their own way. These seven stories move from epic memory to overflowing pots, winter sweets, bonfires, kites and shared harvest feasts.

One turning point, many stories

Makar Sankranti is unusual among major Indian festivals because it follows the solar calendar. It marks the sun's transition into Makara, or Capricorn, and is associated with Uttarayan, the northward course of the sun.

The astronomical moment becomes a cultural bridge. Across regions, people express it through gratitude for harvest, respect for sunlight, community meals, acts of goodwill and the hope that the year's path is growing brighter.

Seven traditions

Regional stories of the turning sun

Different names, foods and customs mark the same seasonal change, revealing a festival that is both local and shared.

One sun, one season

What ties the traditions together

The stories differ in form: one begins with astronomy, one with epic memory, and others with pots, kites, fires, sweets and village feasts. Yet each treats the season as a threshold where nature, work and community come into balance.

Harvest and gratitude, renewal and light, sweetness and goodwill, community and celebration—these shared themes allow one solar turning point to become many meaningful festivals.

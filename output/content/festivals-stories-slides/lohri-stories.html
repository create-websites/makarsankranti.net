Stories behind Punjab's bonfire festival

Lohri Festival Stories

A single winter night carries a folk hero's courage, a farmer's gratitude, family blessings and songs passed from door to door. These seven traditions gather around one warming fire.

One bonfire, many stories

Lohri is rooted in Punjab's seasonal rhythm, but its meaning reaches beyond the harvest. The festival remembers Dulla Bhatti, welcomes newlyweds and babies, sends children singing through the neighborhood and turns a communal bonfire into a place of prayer.

Folk memory and agricultural life meet in a celebration built for participation. Families do not simply hear the stories; they sing them, circle the fire, share winter foods and carry them into the next generation.

Seven traditions

The stories that gather around the fire

Each tradition adds a distinct voice to Lohri while keeping community, gratitude and renewal at the center.

One fire, one spirit

What ties the traditions together

Lohri's stories move between legend, harvest, family and faith. Dulla Bhatti represents courage and generosity; the Rabi fields invite gratitude; first celebrations welcome new life; songs connect generations; and the bonfire gathers every theme into one visible center.

The festival is built through participation. Circling the fire, offering food, singing together and sharing blessings turn memory into community—and make renewal something people create together.

Stories behind the season

Christmas Stories

Christmas carries scripture, legend and history at once. From a manger in Bethlehem to an unexpected pause in wartime, these seven stories reveal how hope, generosity, peace and wonder became part of the season.

One season, many stories

The Christmas story begins with the birth of Jesus, but the season’s wider traditions have also been shaped by saints, folk figures, poems, popular art and moments of humanity in difficult times.

Some accounts are sacred, some legendary and some historical. Read together, they show how communities in different places use story to express the same enduring hopes: light in darkness, generosity without reward and peace among people.

Seven Christmas tales

Sacred beginnings, legends and history

Each account contributes a distinct layer to the season, from the Gospel narratives to traditions that developed across cultures and centuries.

One season, one spirit

Why these stories endure

The stories come from different kinds of sources, so they should not all be read in the same way. The Nativity belongs to Christian scripture; St. Nicholas is a historical figure surrounded by tradition; Santa Claus and La Befana developed through folklore; and the 1914 truce belongs to modern history.

Yet each carries a recognizable Christmas value: hope and light, generosity, peace and goodwill, or the wonder that makes room for joy. Their differences make the season richer while their shared themes keep it connected.

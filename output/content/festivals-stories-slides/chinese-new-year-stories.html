Spring Festival folklore

Chinese New Year Stories

Behind the fireworks, red envelopes and family feasts are stories of courage, cleverness, protection and renewal. Explore seven traditions that help explain why the Spring Festival looks—and feels—the way it does.

One festival, many stories

Chinese New Year, also called the Spring Festival, is more than a date on the calendar. Its customs carry memories of legendary beasts, heavenly races, watchful household gods and families returning home.

Some accounts belong to folklore rather than recorded history, yet their symbols remain easy to recognize: red for protection and good fortune, shared food for togetherness, and light for a hopeful path into the year ahead.

The legends behind the celebration

Each story offers a memorable explanation for a custom, symbol or shared value associated with the Lunar New Year season.

A shared spirit

Why these stories still matter

These stories differ in tone—some are about monsters or gods, while others center on food, cleaning or light. Together, they turn the New Year into a passage from fear to protection, distance to reunion, and the weight of the past to the possibility of renewal.

That is why the customs remain meaningful even when people interpret the legends differently. Each ritual gives families a practical way to express hope: prepare carefully, gather generously, remember one another and begin again.

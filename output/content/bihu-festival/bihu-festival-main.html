Bihu Festival: The Heartbeat of Assam

Bihu is not a single celebration but a yearly cycle of three festivals woven into Assamese life. Rongali welcomes spring and the New Year, Kongali turns toward prayer and crop protection, and Bhogali celebrates the completed harvest with food, fire and community.

The Three Bihus of Assam

Each Bihu reflects a different stage of the farming year and a different emotional tone—from joyful renewal, to restraint and prayer, to post-harvest abundance.

Mid-April

Rongali (Bohag) Bihu

Festival of merriment and the Assamese New Year

Rongali Bihu welcomes spring and begins a fresh agricultural season. It is the most energetic of the three Bihus, marked by music, dance, shared celebration and community bonding.

  • 🎶 Bihu Dance & Husori: lively dance and singing traditions that bring households and communities together.
  • 🐂 Goru Bihu: cattle are honored for their vital role in farming.
  • 🧣 Gamosa: the red-and-white woven cloth is exchanged as a sign of love, respect and Assamese identity.
  • 🍚 Pitha & Laru: rice cakes and traditional sweets are prepared and shared.

Mid-October

Kongali (Kati) Bihu

Festival of scarcity, prayer and crop protection

Kongali Bihu arrives during a leaner agricultural period, when granaries may be bare while crops remain in the fields. The mood is quieter, with attention focused on faith, protection and hopes for a fruitful harvest.

  • 🏮 Saki: earthen lamps are lit in and around paddy fields.
  • 🌿 Tulsi worship: families offer prayers at the sacred Tulsi plant.
  • 🙏 Crop prayers: rituals ask for healthy growth and protection from misfortune.
  • 🌾 Guarding fields: traditional practices reflect the need to protect vulnerable crops.

Mid-January

Bhogali (Magh) Bihu

Festival of harvest, abundance and feasting

Bhogali Bihu follows the harvest, when food stores are replenished. It celebrates gratitude, togetherness and the enjoyment of the season's rewards through community meals and fire rituals.

  • 🔥 Meji & Bhelaghar: bamboo-and-thatch structures form part of the celebration.
  • 🍲 Uruka: the festival eve centers on generous community feasts.
  • 🥮 Harvest foods: Pitha, Laru and other rice-based delicacies feature prominently.
  • 🎯 Traditional games: regional celebrations may include community games and contests.
Agricultural calendar

A Festival Cycle Shaped by the Fields

The three Bihus are closely tied to the status of the paddy crop. That connection explains why their atmosphere changes so dramatically through the year.

April • Bohag
New Year and start of the farming cycle. Mood: hope, celebration and renewal.
October • Kati
Crops are growing while stores are lean. Mood: restraint, prayer and protection.
January • Magh
Harvest is complete and granaries are replenished. Mood: gratitude, feasting and community.

Cultural fabric

Dance, Music, Food, Attire and Symbols

Across the three Bihus, Assamese cultural identity is expressed through performance, food, weaving, agricultural rituals and familiar objects.

💃

Bihu Dance & Music

Rongali Bihu is especially associated with spirited group dance, Husori singing and the rhythms of instruments such as the Dhol and Pepa.

🍚

Bihu Cuisine

Pitha, Laru, Chira, Doi and Gur are recurring festival foods, with post-harvest Bhogali Bihu placing special emphasis on communal eating.

🧣

Attire & Gamosa

Women traditionally wear Mekhela Sador, while men may wear Dhoti with Gamosa. The red-and-white Gamosa carries strong associations with honor, affection and Assamese identity.

🥁

Cultural Artifacts

Dhol, Pepa, Japi, Saki and Meji each represent a recognizable piece of the Bihu cultural landscape.

Food traditions

A Taste of Bihu

Bihu food reflects Assam's rice-growing culture. Rice preparations and sweets appear throughout the festival cycle, and Bhogali Bihu turns that culinary heritage into a central community celebration.

Pitha

Traditional rice cakes prepared in multiple sweet and savory forms.

Laru

Sweet balls often made with coconut or sesame.

Chira, Doi & Gur

Flattened rice served with curd and jaggery as a traditional combination.

Uruka Feast

The eve of Bhogali Bihu brings people together around generous community meals.

Quick answers

Bihu Festival FAQ

What is Bihu?

Bihu is a cycle of three major Assamese festivals connected with the agricultural year: Rongali or Bohag Bihu, Kongali or Kati Bihu, and Bhogali or Magh Bihu.

What are the three Bihu festivals?

Rongali (Bohag) Bihu is observed in mid-April, Kongali (Kati) Bihu in mid-October, and Bhogali (Magh) Bihu in mid-January.

Which Bihu marks the Assamese New Year?

Rongali, also called Bohag Bihu, marks the Assamese New Year and celebrates the arrival of spring.

What is special about Bhogali Bihu?

Bhogali or Magh Bihu follows the harvest and emphasizes community feasts, Uruka, Meji and Bhelaghar, along with traditional rice-based foods.

What happens during Kongali Bihu?

Kongali or Kati Bihu is more solemn. Families light earthen lamps, worship Tulsi and pray for the protection and healthy growth of crops.

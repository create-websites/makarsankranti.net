A few things to know

Your Lohri questions, answered.

What is Lohri?

Lohri is a winter festival especially associated with Punjab and Punjabi communities. Bonfires, folk songs, shared sweets and family gatherings express gratitude, togetherness and hope for the season ahead.

What foods are associated with Lohri?

Sesame (til), jaggery (gur), rewri, gajak, peanuts and popcorn are familiar festive foods. A meal may include sarson da saag and makki di roti; the menu guide offers more dishes and drinks.

Why is Dulla Bhatti remembered at Lohri?

Punjabi folklore remembers Dulla Bhatti as a protector of vulnerable young women. Lohri songs, including versions of Sundar Mundariye, keep these stories of courage and generosity alive.

Is Lohri the same as Makar Sankranti?

They are distinct celebrations that occur close together in January. Lohri is especially associated with Punjabi bonfire gatherings; Makar Sankranti is marked by varied regional customs. The history guide explores the connection.

How can children take part in Lohri?

Children can make paper bonfires, design greeting cards, listen to folk stories and learn clapping rhythms or simple dance steps. The kids’ guide provides an activity checklist, and a paper or LED display can be used for an indoor celebration.

What makes a first Lohri special?

Many families mark the first Lohri after a birth or marriage with extra blessings, gifts, songs and a gathering. Customs vary by family, and the first-Lohri guide offers ideas for both babies and newlyweds.

Traditions vary across regions, communities and households. This collection offers an introduction and celebration ideas, with room for your own family’s customs.

When is Golu in 2026?

For a Tamil Nadu/Chennai reference, Navaratri: and the main Golu season: runs from Sunday, October 11 through Tuesday, October 20, 2026. Families may begin arranging dolls before the first day, and household ritual schedules can vary by local Panchang and family tradition.

Saturday • October 10

Mahalaya Amavasai

A natural preparation marker for many families. The Tamil festival calendar places Mahalaya Amavasai on October 10, the day before Navaratri begins.

Sunday • October 11

Navaratri & Golu begin

The Tamil Nadu HR&CE calendar lists Navaratri beginning October 11, 2026. Golu displays are commonly opened for worship and visits from this period.

October 11–20

Main Golu season

Use these days for the doll display, family visits, music, devotional activities, sundal and storytelling. Exact household observances vary.

Tuesday • October 20

Vijayadashami

For Chennai, Vijayadashami falls on October 20, 2026. It marks the close of Navaratri and is associated with auspicious beginnings and learning.

Date note: Panchang dates and ritual timings are location-sensitive. This page uses Tamil Nadu/Chennai as a reference. Sources: Tamil Nadu HR&CE festival calendar, Drik Panchang : Chennai Vijayadashami, and Drik Panchang Tamil calendar. Follow your local Panchang for exact timings.

Core idea

What is Golu and why is it significant?

Golu is a South Indian Navaratri tradition in which families arrange dolls and figurines on stepped displays. It is known as Bommai Golu or Kolu in Tamil communities, Bombe Habba in Karnataka, and Bommala Koluvu in parts of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana. The display can bring together devotion, mythology, craft, family memory, hospitality and storytelling.

DevotionDeities and sacred stories give the display a spiritual center during Navaratri.
StorytellingRamayana, Krishna, village, wedding and temple scenes let families pass stories to children visually.
Craft & heritageClay dolls, Marapachi figures and inherited sets preserve artisan traditions and family memory.
CommunityVisits, songs, haldi-kumkum, sundal and return gifts turn the home display into a social tradition.
LearningSaraswati worship and Vijayadashami connect the season with education, music and new beginnings.
Regional varietyNo single arrangement defines every household; family, regional and sampradaya practices can differ.

A useful distinction

Golu is a Navaratri tradition, not a Diwali ritual

Golu usually takes place during Navaratri and concludes around Vijayadashami. Diwali follows later in the festive calendar. Because the celebrations are relatively close, they can feel like part of one long festive season, but their rituals, stories and dates are distinct.

For first-time hosts, a simple display can be enough. Start with a stable set of steps, a small group of meaningful dolls, a lamp or puja space if that is part of your family practice, and room for visitors. The collection can grow over time.

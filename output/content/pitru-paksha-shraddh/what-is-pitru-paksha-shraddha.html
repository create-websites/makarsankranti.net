Pitru Paksha guide • Meaning, terminology, overlap and common traditions
Understanding the tradition

What Is Pitru Paksha — and How Is It Different From Shraddha?

Pitru Paksha and Shraddha are closely connected, but they are not interchangeable terms. Pitru Paksha describes a sacred period in the Hindu lunar calendar devoted to remembering ancestors. Shraddha describes the ritual tradition of making offerings and honoring the departed — during Pitru Paksha and at other times as well.

ॐ पितृभ्यो नमः
Pitru Paksha

Pitru refers to ancestors; Paksha is a lunar fortnight. Pitru Paksha is the annual Krishna Paksha period in which ancestral remembrance and Shraddha observances receive special emphasis.

Shraddha / Shradh

Shraddha is an ancestral rite or family observance involving remembrance, offerings and acts performed for deceased relatives. Its forms vary across regions, lineages and family traditions.

The simplest way to remember the relationship: Pitru Paksha is primarily the time period; Shraddha is primarily the ritual observance. Many Shraddha rites are performed during Pitru Paksha, which is why the terms frequently appear together.

What is Pitru Paksha?

Pitru Paksha is an annual period in the Hindu lunar calendar dedicated to remembering and honoring ancestors. It is associated with the waning half of the lunar month — Krishna Paksha — leading toward Amavasya, the new moon.

The name combines pitru (ancestor or forefather) and paksha (a lunar half-month or fortnight). In North Indian Purnimanta calendar naming, the period is associated with Krishna Paksha of Ashwin; in South Indian Amanta naming, the same lunar days are associated with Krishna Paksha of Bhadrapada. The month label differs, but the observance can refer to the same underlying lunar sequence.

The period is also commonly called Shraddha Paksha or Mahalaya Paksha in different communities. Practices and terminology vary by region, language and family tradition.

Calendar note: Pitru Paksha does not have fixed Gregorian dates. Its civil dates shift every year because it follows lunar tithis. Exact dates and ritual timings can also vary by location and tithi boundaries.

What is Shraddha?

Śrāddha is a Hindu ancestral rite traditionally performed in memory of the deceased. It may involve offerings of water and food, remembrance of ancestors, feeding others, charity, prayer and rites conducted according to family or regional custom.

Religious reference works describe Shraddha as part of a much broader ancestral-rite tradition that is not limited to Pitru Paksha. Shraddha may be associated with post-funeral rites, annual death-anniversary observances based on the lunar tithi, and the annual Pitru Paksha period.

Common ritual vocabulary includes tarpana (offerings of water), pinda daan (offering pinda, commonly prepared from rice or other prescribed ingredients), and food or donations offered in memory of ancestors. Not every family performs every element, and procedures can vary substantially.

Spelling note: English usage includes Shraddha, Shradh and Śrāddha. Do not confuse the ritual term śrāddha with the related Sanskrit concept śraddhā, meaning faith or trust.

How Pitru Paksha and Shraddha overlap

The overlap is strong because Pitru Paksha is one of the most prominent annual periods for performing Shraddha. But the two concepts sit at different levels.

1. Lunar periodPitru Paksha identifies the annual ancestral fortnight.
2. Ancestor tithiFamilies identify the relevant lunar death tithi when known.
3. ShraddhaThe ancestral rite is performed according to family and regional practice.
4. Final AmavasyaSarva Pitru Amavasya concludes the principal sequence.

A useful analogy is that Pitru Paksha is like a season of observance, while Shraddha is an observance performed within that season. The analogy is imperfect, but it helps explain why a person may say “we are doing Shraddha during Pitru Paksha.”

Shraddha, however, is not confined to these days. Annual ancestral rites may also be observed on the deceased person's lunar death anniversary, and post-death rites have their own Shraddha-related traditions.

Why the death tithi matters

Traditional Pitru Paksha calendars list Shraddha by tithi rather than by the ancestor's Gregorian death date. For example, a person whose death is remembered on Navami tithi may be honored on Navami Shraddha during Pitru Paksha.

This is one reason two families can perform Shraddha on different civil dates in the same year. Their ancestral tithis may be different — and the mapping of a tithi to a local civil day can also depend on location and the tithi's start and end times.

When the exact tithi is not known, many traditions give particular importance to the concluding Amavasya, commonly called Sarva Pitru Amavasya. Family custom should take precedence over a generic online schedule.

Purnima Shraddha and Sarva Pitru Amavasya

Purnima Shraddha

Purnima Shraddha is frequently shown immediately before the main Krishna Paksha sequence. Some published calendars group it with the annual Shraddha list, while stricter definitions distinguish the full-moon observance from the fifteen lunar days of Pitru Paksha itself. This is why different sites may appear to give a slightly different “start date.”

Sarva Pitru Amavasya

The Amavasya at the end of Pitru Paksha is widely known as Sarva Pitru Amavasya, Mahalaya Amavasya or by related regional names. It is a major concluding observance associated with remembering ancestors collectively and is especially significant in many families when a specific ancestral tithi is unknown or was not otherwise observed.

Common elements of Shraddha observance

There is no single universal household procedure. Regional schools, priests, lineages and family customs differ. Still, several themes recur across traditional descriptions.

SankalpaA formal statement of intention used in many Hindu rituals.
TarpanaWater offerings made in remembrance of ancestors, often with prescribed mantras and ingredients.
Pinda DaanOffering of pinda, commonly rice-based ritual portions, according to tradition.
Food offeringPrepared food may be offered symbolically and later shared according to family custom.
Feeding and charityFeeding Brahmins, guests, needy people, animals or making donations may form part of observance in some traditions.
RemembranceRecalling ancestors by name, lineage or intention is central to the commemorative purpose.
Practical caution: Detailed ritual rules can be tradition-specific. A family priest, knowledgeable elder or local Panchang is more appropriate than a generic web page for deciding exact mantras, sequence and timing.

Why Gaya, Prayagraj, Varanasi and other sacred places are associated with Shraddha

Hindu pilgrimage traditions have long connected sacred rivers and tirthas with ancestral rites. Places such as Gaya, Varanasi (Kashi), Prayagraj, Haridwar and other pilgrimage centers are strongly associated with pinda daan, tarpana and remembrance of ancestors.

These pilgrimage practices are distinct from the basic definition of Pitru Paksha: a family does not have to travel to a famous tirtha for the period to be meaningful. Many households observe Shraddha at home or locally, following their own tradition.

Frequently asked questions

Are Pitru Paksha and Shraddha the same thing?

No. Pitru Paksha is the annual lunar period associated with ancestral remembrance. Shraddha is the rite or observance performed for ancestors. Shraddha is strongly associated with Pitru Paksha but is not limited to it.

Why do people call Pitru Paksha “Shraddha”?

Because Shraddha is the central ancestral observance associated with the period, everyday speech often uses “Shraddha days” or “Shradh Paksha” as shorthand for Pitru Paksha.

Is Pitru Paksha always 15 days?

It is fundamentally a lunar-paksha observance, but civil-calendar presentations can look like 15 or 16 listed dates depending on whether Purnima Shraddha is displayed with the sequence and how tithis fall across sunrise and civil dates.

Do all ancestors have Shraddha on the same day?

No. Many calendars assign Shraddha according to the ancestor's death tithi. Sarva Pitru Amavasya provides a widely observed concluding day with broader ancestral significance.

Can Shraddha be performed outside Pitru Paksha?

Yes. Hindu ancestral-rite traditions include annual death-tithi observances and post-funeral Shraddha rites in addition to Pitru Paksha.

Why do dates vary by city?

Tithis are astronomical-lunar divisions whose start and end times can interact differently with local sunrise and local civil time. A location-specific Panchang should be used for exact observance dates and timings.
